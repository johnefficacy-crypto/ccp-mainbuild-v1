## ACC-001 · L2 · medium · Accounting equation

Mehar Stores begins with total assets of ₹5,00,000 and outside liabilities of ₹1,80,000. During the week:

1. Goods costing ₹40,000 sold on credit at a mark-up of 25% on cost.
2. Paid a creditor ₹30,000.
3. Proprietor took goods costing ₹8,000 for personal use.
4. Salary of ₹6,000 became outstanding.

Applying the accounting equation, closing capital is:

- **A.** ₹3,32,000  _(error: drawings added to capital (sign reversed))_
- **B.** ₹3,22,000  _(error: outstanding salary ignored (accrued expense missed))_
- **C.** ₹3,16,000 ✅
- **D.** ₹3,56,000  _(error: entire sale value added to capital instead of the profit element)_

**Working**

1. Opening capital = 5,00,000 − 1,80,000 = 3,20,000
2. Credit sale: profit 50,000 − 40,000 = 10,000 raises capital
3. Paying a creditor reduces assets and liabilities equally — no effect on capital
4. Drawings −8,000; outstanding salary is an expense −6,000
5. Closing capital = 3,20,000 + 10,000 − 8,000 − 6,000 = 3,16,000

**Formula:** Capital = Assets − Liabilities; ΔCapital = Profit − Drawings  
**Trap:** Only the profit on a sale (not the sale value) changes capital; a creditor payment never does.
