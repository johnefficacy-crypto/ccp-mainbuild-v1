## MGT-009 · L3 · hard · Levels of management

An operations centre has 4,096 operative staff. Every manager (at every level) supervises exactly 8 subordinates, up to a single head. If the span of control is widened uniformly to 16, what happens to the number of managerial posts and the number of managerial levels?

- **A.** Managers fall by 312; levels remain 4  _(error: ignores that a wider span collapses a level)_
- **B.** Managers fall by 256; levels fall from 4 to 3  _(error: compares only the first-line supervisory layer)_
- **C.** Managers fall by 293; levels fall from 4 to 2  _(error: assumes doubling the span halves both managers and levels)_
- **D.** Managers fall by 312; levels fall from 4 to 3 ✅

**Working**

1. Span 8: 4096/8 = 512, 512/8 = 64, 64/8 = 8, 8/8 = 1 → 585 managers in 4 levels.
2. Span 16: 4096/16 = 256, 256/16 = 16, 16/16 = 1 → 273 managers in 3 levels.
3. Reduction = 585 − 273 = 312.

**Formula:** Managers at level k = Operatives ÷ span^k; sum over levels until 1  
**Trap:** A wider span gives a flatter structure — fewer posts AND fewer levels; the reduction is not a simple halving.
